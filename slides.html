01 · The need

Why build agriSense?

Farm decisions depend on local conditions, but weather, farm records, and practical guidance are often spread across different places.

01

Weather changes plans

Rain, heat, and wind can affect when to irrigate, plant, protect animals, or work in the field.

02

Farm records matter

Tasks, expenses, harvests, inventory, and animal observations are easier to use when kept together.

03

One farm, many needs

Farmers may manage crops, livestock, poultry, fisheries, or beekeeping—not just one crop.

System goal: bring a farmer’s profile, local weather, practical checklists, and farm records into one place.

What can a farmer do?

Create a farm profile

Register, sign in, and save location, farm activity, crops or animal groups, soil, and growth stage.

04 · PHP request flow

From sign-in to a useful dashboard

  1. Receive the requestThe browser sends a form or page request to a PHP entry point such as index.php or dashboard.php.
  2. Check identity and inputPHP checks the session, validates submitted data, verifies CSRF tokens, and applies login throttling where needed.
  3. Load farm and weather dataReusable PHP modules fetch the farmer profile and retrieve weather from the cache or weather service.
  4. Apply system logicPHP builds weather-aware tasks and optional crop or yield estimates for that farmer.
  5. Return the responsePHP renders the dashboard as HTML or serves JSON to the farm-journal interface.
PHP’s role: connect the user request to authentication, data, business rules, and the final response.

07 · Weather and guidance

Turn local conditions into practical checks

01

Choose a place

The farmer’s saved weather town or region determines the forecast location.

15

Cache for minutes

Weather snapshots are cached for 15 minutes to reduce repeated external requests.

→

Build a checklist

PHP combines weather with farm activity, soil, and growth stage to suggest what to check today.

Models are supporting tools

Crop fit, yield estimates, and the PHP advisory model are separate from the core request flow; rule-based guidance can cover unavailable or low-confidence advice.

Use local judgment

The dashboard helps prioritize checks. It does not replace field inspection, soil tests, veterinary care, or agricultural extension advice.

08 · Farm journal

Useful records, even with patchy connectivity

Records for the whole farm

  • Tasks, reminders, and field activities
  • Income, expenses, and harvests
  • Crop problem reports and photos
  • Animal activity and input inventory

How offline support works

  • Browser stores journal edits in IndexedDB.
  • Each record is associated with its farmer.
  • When online, JavaScript sends queued changes to the PHP API.
  • PHP validates the request and saves it to the database.
Key idea: the browser handles the offline queue; PHP remains responsible for validating and saving records on the server.
